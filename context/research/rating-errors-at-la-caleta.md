# One tide setting misranked La Caleta

**The app's own code, run on the Open-Meteo data for 14 November 2026, gives La Caleta q = 1.16 at 11:00 and q = 3.95 at 13:00. That matches the 1.2 and 4.0 you saw, and almost all of the gap comes from one parameter: the spot's static `'mid-high'` tide preference.** At 11:00 the tide factor is 0.34; at 13:00 it is 1.00. That is a ×2.9 swing, against ×1.2 from wind, while size and period cancel out. None of the wave models shows a swell drop at noon. The swell input changes by about 3 % between 11:00 and 13:00, and the wind actually *eased* at 13:00. So the "nearly flat 12:00–14:00" you observed is missing from the data the app sees. Tide is the only variable that changed a lot: low water came at about 10:00 and high water at about 16:00. That made your good window low tide +1 to +2 h and your dead window the fast mid-flood, the reverse of what `'mid-high'` rewards. The guides disagree among themselves ("all tides", "mid to high", "best at high tide"), and the app turned that weak prior into a hard veto. Three further defects made things worse. The tide penalty is steep, normalised per calendar day and blind to trend. The size rules have **no lower bound tied to the surfer**, so 0.7 m at Flag Beach counts as "Right in your range" for someone comfortable to 1.8 m. And a paddle-out rule halves La Caleta for you all day. In order, the fixes are: a size-fit curve with a floor and a "progress" goal; a softer tide prior whose strength depends on how good the evidence is; tide preferences learned from your own sessions; and fixes to tide normalisation, swell window, sample points and best-window selection. Even with all of them, the model data alone would still pick 13:00 over 11:00. Only local knowledge, learned from logged sessions, can get the timing right at this spot.

The 3.4× ratio between 13:00 and 11:00 splits into **tide ×2.92, wind ×1.21, size ×1.03 and period ×0.94**. Without the tide factor, 11:00 scores 3.38 and 13:00 scores 3.95, nearly the same. Neither the exposure factor (1.0 for both trains at both hours), the gain (0.77) nor the sample point differs between the two hours. No time-zone, off-by-one or datum bug is involved. `timezone=auto` returns Atlantic/Canary at UTC+0, and only the relative tide stage is used, so the MSL-versus-chart-datum offset doesn't matter.

### The tide axis is right; the preference is wrong for this day

The model tide runs about 25 minutes ahead of the published table, but its range matches (1.17 m modelled from low to high against 1.13 m in the table). So the app placed 11:00 and 13:00 correctly on the tide curve. The problem is what it does with that position. Four choices in `fTide()` and `tideInfo()` compound:

1. **The band comes from conflicting guides.** The seed says `tide: 'mid-high'` (norm 0.4–1.0). The guides disagree. fuerteventuraplayas says "all tides" ([fuerteventuraplayas](https://www.fuerteventuraplayas.com/en/?p=7880)), and so does surf-forecast's same-coordinate "Mechihonas" entry ([surf-forecast](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Mechihonas)). Mondo says mid to high ([Mondo.surf](https://www.mondo.surf/surf-spot/caleta/guide/1525)). vvanat.fr and Planet Surf Camps say "best at high tide", in near-identical wording that suggests they are not independent ([vvanat.fr](https://vvanat.fr/blog/carte-spot-surf-corralejo/); [Planet Surf Camps](https://planetsurfcamps.co.uk/surf-camps-canary-islands/surf-camp-fuerteventura/surfspots-fuerteventura)). The app's own texts don't match the seed either. The seed description says "best on a mid-rising tide", and the guide in `src/data/spotGuides.ts` says "Softer and slower at high tide, and surf schools use it". That last line, if anything, points towards lower water for real waves.
2. **The penalty is steep.** `1 − dist × 2.2` with a floor of 0.3 reaches the floor 0.32 norm units outside the band. A 0.30 shortfall (11:00) costs ×0.34, more than any realistic wind that day. One sparsely sourced attribute can therefore swamp all of the wave signal.
3. **Normalisation is per calendar day, night included.** The day's range runs from −0.92 m to +0.47 m (the 03:19 night high). The afternoon high reached only +0.24 m, or norm 0.83. A level of −0.23 m, below mean sea level, counted as "mid" and got the full 1.0.
4. **Trend and timing are ignored.** `tideTrend` is computed but never reaches `quality()`, although the seed and guide texts talk about "rising". Open-Meteo values are instantaneous at the top of the hour, but `recommendSpotDay`, the daylight test and `surfedHourIdx` treat hour m as the bucket [m, m+60). The 13:00 value (norm 0.50) therefore stands for an hour in which the tide reached 0.69.

The likeliest explanation for your session is depth over the reef. By the rule of twelfths, the water rose about 0.3 m per hour between 12:00 and 14:00 and stood roughly 0.9 m deeper at 14:00 than at 11:00 (inference from the table's 1.16 m flood range). Video studies of a fixed reef find the rate of rides "inversely proportional to tide level", as depth-limited breaking predicts ([MDPI Wave Peel Tracking](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/17/3372)). In a moderate, decaying swell, a sheltered inside reef that fills in can stop all but the sets from breaking. That is a hypothesis, not a fact, and it rests on one session. Inconsistent sets cannot be ruled out. But it is the only explanation consistent with every data source, and it suggests the guides' "mid–high" advice applies to bigger swells or to the outer reef.

### How the prominent systems handle tide

None of the big services rates tide automatically. Surfline's model rating "uses surf height and wind conditions" only, and Surfline admits it can miss "a very high tide that slows conditions at tide-dependent spots". Tide enters only when a human forecaster overrides the model, and only forecasters can award Good or Epic ([Surfline What's New, Apr 2025](https://www.surfline.com/lp/whatsnew/releases/apr-25); [Surfline Support](https://support.surfline.com/hc/en-us/articles/36277684017819-Surf-Ratings-Colors)). The surf-forecast.com FAQ describes stars from swell size and period, cut by onshore wind, and never mentions tide ([Surf-Forecast FAQ](https://www.surf-forecast.com/pages/faq)). Magicseaweed's solid-and-faded stars were swell minus a wind deduction, again with no documented tide input ([MSW rating doc](https://de.magicseaweed.com/docs/developers/59/msw-rating/9913/)). Surf Captain shows tide for information only ([Surf Captain FAQ](https://surfcaptain.com/faq)). Open-source raters give tide about 15 % in an additive mix ([romain-ollivier/Surf_Project](https://github.com/romain-ollivier/Surf_Project)) or leave it out.

So your multiplicative tide factor is a feature the commercial automated tiers lack, and the right one for reefs that really do shut off. But it needs what Surfline gets from its forecasters: a correction channel. In this app that channel can only be your own sessions. Several small apps (Buio, Session, Spotadvisor, Lazy Surfer) market exactly this: learning which swell, wind and tide suit you at each spot. None publishes its method ([Buio](https://buioapp.com); [Spotadvisor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1513801699); [Lazy Surfer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lazy-surfer/id1450887020)).

## Wind, swell window and sample points add smaller, fixable errors

**Wind did not cause the ranking, but its inputs are weak.** With `facing` 345°, the NE trade wind (35–41°) is cross-onshore, and `fWind` cuts the score by 50–60%. From 14:00 the wind veered NNE and became onshore. That matches the guides, which list only S/SE/E or SW as good winds ([Mondo.surf](https://www.mondo.surf/surf-spot/caleta/guide/1525); [vvanat.fr](https://vvanat.fr/blog/carte-spot-surf-corralejo/)). Between 11:00 and 13:00, though, the modelled wind eased from 17 to 14 km/h, so it favoured 13:00. The weaknesses lie in the data. The forecast API returned a grid cell at 28.69, −13.94, about 9 km south of the coastal sample point, which places it over land near Lajares (inferred from the coordinates; Open-Meteo's forecast API picks a land cell by default). Gusts of 27–29 km/h are fetched but never used. And ECMWF backs the wind towards N earlier than the best-match model does ([Open-Meteo Forecast API](https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=28.751&longitude=-13.923&hourly=wind_speed_10m,wind_direction_10m,wind_gusts_10m&models=ecmwf_ifs025,icon_eu,meteofrance_seamless&timezone=UTC&start_date=2026-11-14&end_date=2026-11-14&wind_speed_unit=kn)). This matches common practice: surf-forecast.com cuts the rating "in proportion to the wind speed" for onshore wind, and MSW faded its stars the same way ([Surf-Forecast FAQ](https://www.surf-forecast.com/pages/faq); [MSW](https://de.magicseaweed.com/docs/developers/59/msw-rating/9913/)). None of them publishes angle bins or gust handling. Surfline admits its model misses "residual chop" from earlier winds ([Surfline Apr 2025](https://www.surfline.com/lp/whatsnew/releases/apr-25)).

**The swell window lets a W swell count in full at an N-facing bay.** La Caleta has `idealSwellDir` 315° and `swellWindow` 70°. With the 0.55 core fraction in `exposure()`, everything from 276° to 354° counts as fully exposed. The 0.6 m W train from 276° therefore counts as much as the NNW train, although the guides want NW–N swell. Mondo gives 293°–68° and surf-forecast "best NNW" ([Mondo.surf](https://www.mondo.surf/surf-spot/caleta/guide/1525); [surf-forecast Mechihonas](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Mechihonas)), and one guide calls the spot "sheltered within a small bay" ([fuerteventuraplayas](https://www.fuerteventuraplayas.com/en/?p=7880)). That train adds about 0.19 m to `hb` (1.62 → 1.81 m at 11:00, by my calculation with the app's formulas). It barely moves `fSize`, but it pushes the surfer-facing height into the head-high band, where the suitability cliffs bite (see the size section). Surf Captain also builds height from the most energetic partitions rather than one lump ([Surf Captain FAQ](https://surfcaptain.com/faq)). None of the commercial documentation describes its swell-window masking.

**Flag Beach's wave data probably comes from the wrong side of Isla de Lobos.** `samplePoint()` in `src/lib/geo.ts` moves 2.5 km from the spot along `facing` (55°) and rounds to 0.01°. That gives 28.74, −13.82, approximately on or next to Lobos. MFWAM's nearest valid cell was 28.708, −13.708, about 11 km east, across the Bocaina strait. Flag Beach's modelled Hs of 0.48–0.56 m may not represent the beach. This is inferred from geography; Lobos's outline was not checked against a map. The general problem is that a fixed 2.5 km offset along `facing` can land on an island or a headland, and the app never checks how far away the returned grid cell is.

## Thigh-high waves scored as ideal for a head-high surfer

The Flag Beach message comes from a rule that only looks upwards. In `suitability()` (`src/lib/rating.ts`), `H ≤ 0.8·C` returns "good" with the text "Right in your range". There is no lower bound tied to the surfer. The only small-wave check is the board minimum (`BOARDS` in `src/lib/skill.ts`: mid-length 0.45 m, shortboard 0.7 m), which 0.65 m passes. The spot factor `fSize` is relative to each spot's own range, so 0.6–0.7 m scores 0.83–0.89 at Flag Beach (range 0.4–2 m) but only 0.30–0.45 at La Caleta (0.8–3 m). For your profile, Flag Beach therefore beat La Caleta at every hour from 11:00: For-you 2.93 against 0.58 at 11:00, and 4.06 against 1.98 at 13:00 (reproduction).

Three other rules pushed in the same direction for you:

- **Paddle-out rule.** It halved La Caleta all day. Any hazard matching /rip|current/ with `paddleOut ≤ 1` and `H > 0.8·C` is "challenging" (×0.5). The stage-4 default `paddleOut` is 1, and you had raised `comfort` to 1.8 m without revisiting it. The "rips" tag itself is doubtful for a bay that Mondo calls suitable for all levels, with schools in the water ([Mondo.surf](https://www.mondo.surf/surf-spot/caleta/guide/1525)).
- **Size cliff.** With `max` left at the stage-4 default of 1.5 m, `M = max(p.max, C)` collapses to 1.8 m. Any `hb` that rounds to 1.9 m then reads "Bigger than anything you've surfed" and becomes unsuitable (score 0). That happened at 07:00 and 10:00, so For-you jumped 0 → 1.24 → 0.66 → 0 between 07:00 and 10:00 on 5 cm model changes. `skill.ts` has a warning text for `max < comfort`, but the engine silently removes the stretch band instead of asking.
- **Spot description.** La Caleta's `power: 'punchy'` and `peel: 'fast'` tags sit uneasily with guides that describe a sheltered bay for all levels. They don't affect stage 4 today, but they would for stages ≤ 3.

### What coaching practice and learning science say

Every progression ladder found puts a stage-4 intermediate well above thigh-high. Urbnsurf's wave-pool settings run beginner progressive at 0.5–1.0 m, progressive turns at 1.0 m, intermediate at about 1.5 m and advanced at 1.6–2.0 m ([Urbnsurf Sydney](https://urbnsurf.com/sydney/?p=1393); [Urbnsurf Melbourne Progressive Turns](https://urbnsurf.com/melbourne/?p=11645); [Urbnsurf Melbourne Advanced](https://urbnsurf.com/melbourne/?p=120)). Alaïa Bay's smallest "Cruiser" wave is for pop-ups and board control, intermediate sessions are waist to chest, and head-high is where progressing shortboarders learn turns ([Alaïa Bay Level Guide](https://alaia.ch/bay/en/news-post/level-guide/)). Surf Maroc defines level 4 as "head-high waves at a beach break without feeling out of your depth" ([Surf Maroc](https://surfmaroc.com/en/whats-my-surf-level/)). Coaches warn that on "soft mushy waves that are barely standing up" a hard bottom turn just bogs the rail ([Ombe](https://ombe.co/guides/intermediate-surfer-not-improving)). The counterpoint is that small mushy waves still teach speed generation and pocket use ([Boardcave](https://www.boardcave.com/surf-school/intermediate-how-to-surf-part-2)), which argues for a ramp rather than a zero.

Learning science points to the same shape. The challenge point framework holds that learning peaks at an optimal difficulty that rises with skill, and that learning potential is low at low difficulty because little information is available ([Challenge point framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenge_point_framework)). Flow theory adds boredom below the match and anxiety above it ([Patagonia](https://www.patagonia.com/stories/flow/story-18147.html)). Among apps, the patterns are a skill gate against too-hard conditions (Swellify, which is what this app does) or a personal "best wave-height range" learned from rated sessions, which yields a floor naturally ([Swellify](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/swellify/id6470770160); [Surf Log](https://www.coddykit.com/apps/surf-log)). Windy stores a per-spot minimum and maximum, not a per-surfer one ([Windy Community](https://community.windy.com/topic/8969/how-to-read-the-surfing-spots)). Surfline documents no skill or size setting at all ([Surfline App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/surfline-wave-surf-reports/id393782096)). No source publishes a numeric curve, so the one proposed below is a design built on these bands, not a validated value.

## The best window inherits every bias and hides its uncertainty

`recommendSpotDay()` (`src/lib/recommend.ts`) scores each daylight hour as For-you = q × `SUIT_FACTOR`, blended with personal history through `predict()` (prior weight `PRIOR_N0` = 2). It then picks the best mean of two contiguous hours. On 14 November that gave **13:00–15:00**, best hour 13:00, for every `nowMin` tested. That is a faithful two-hour argmax of a score whose ranking came from the tide prior. The UI doesn't say that the window is tide-driven, how much better it is than the rest of the day, or that the 11:00 value was one 5 cm swing away from being 0. Two smaller defects add to this. Top-of-hour values stand for the following hour, which matters most for tide, the fastest-changing input (norm 0.50 → 0.69 across the 13:00 "hour"). And the cliffs in the suitability rules make For-you jump between 0 and 1.3 from hour to hour. Surfline, surf-forecast.com and MSW publish no best-time algorithm; the most likely approach is a maximum over hourly ratings, but this is unverified.

The personal-history path already uses tide stage and trend in its similarity distance (`SPOT_FEATURES` in `src/lib/similarity.ts`: tide weight 2, tolerance 0.18; trend weight 1). If you log this session with a high fun rating, similar low-rising days at La Caleta will score higher for you. But that correction reaches only For-you, not the spot rating. It needs logged sessions. And the app has no way to record "13:00 was flat" unless you log a second session, and `calibratedGain` skips sessions marked flat.

## Conclusion

The mistake here wasn't in the arithmetic. It was misplaced confidence. The app combined a well-measured quantity (tide timing) with a poorly sourced belief (which tide La Caleta likes), and the multiplicative design let that belief override every wave signal. That is the very failure Surfline papers over with human forecasters. The lesson for the engine is that each spot attribute needs an evidence weight, and the weakest attributes should be the first ones your sessions are allowed to overwrite. Your sessions are this app's equivalent of a forecaster.

The size complaint has the same root: rules written as safety gates were being used as fit scores. Splitting "is this safe for me?" (gates) from "will this session help me improve?" (a continuous fit with a floor and a goal) fixes Flag Beach without weakening any safety rule. Neither fix can be validated from one day. Freeze 14 November as a test fixture, add the one-tap "check" log so flat hours count as evidence, and judge every tide and size parameter by whether it predicts the next ten sessions better than the last version did.
