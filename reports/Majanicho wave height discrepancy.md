# Hard size veto turns Majanicho uncertainty into exclusion

The app's ~2.5 m "Wave" for Majanicho on 19 Nov 2026 is not the same quantity as the ~1.5 m that other sites show, and most of the gap comes from that difference. The app's "Wave" is an estimated **breaking face height**. surf-forecast.com's 1.5–1.6 m is the **open-water height of the primary swell**, and Windfinder's 2.0–2.1 m is most likely total significant wave height. The one site that also shows face height, Surfline, gives **2.4–3 m at dawn and 1.8–2.4 m for the rest of the day**. Measured against Surfline, the app reads about right at dawn and **0.4–0.7 m too high from mid-morning on**. Three modelling choices cause that excess: the app scales every swell train up to Météo-France's MFWAM, the highest of the three wave models that day. It combines trains in a way that adds a few per cent. And it treats Majanicho as a fully exposed reef with a generic 0.77 gain and no shelter. Depending only on those choices, the noon estimate lands anywhere from **2.24 to 2.86 m**, a spread that straddles the user's 2.2 m limit. The bigger problem is what happens next: the app compares one central number with "Biggest caught" (2.2 m) as a **hard veto**. Every daylight hour scores 0, and the spot drops to the bottom of Explore marked "not for you". It does this even though the app's own ±20 % range and the 0.3 m disagreement between models both reach below 2.2 m. The fix is not to swap face height for raw swell height. That would compare an offshore height with a face-height limit and ignore a real 15–16 s groundswell. Instead, the app should carry model spread through to the decision, exclude a spot only when even the low end of the range is too big, calibrate Majanicho's shelter, and use swell energy as a cross-check when the heights are uncertain.

## Five sources, four different quantities, one consistent ocean

All sources agree on the sea itself. Tomorrow brings a moderate WNW swell plus a long-period NW groundswell, about 2.0–2.3 m of total significant height offshore, with a light NE–E breeze of 7–12 kt and almost no wind sea. The big differences in the headline numbers come from which quantity each site puts up front.

The app rows come from running the app's own `marineHour`, `deriveSeries` and `recommendSpotDay` on the live Open-Meteo responses the app requests.

surf-forecast says outright that its figure is a **"near shore open water"** forecast and that "breaking waves will often be smaller at less exposed spots" ([surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Majanicho/forecasts/latest/six_day)). Its FAQ confirms that swell sizes are open-water heights and that breaking heights are "strongly determined by the local sea bed geometry" ([surf-forecast FAQ](https://www.surf-forecast.com/pages/faq)). The app's "Wave" row, by contrast, carries the tooltip "the face of the waves, from trough to crest", and the legend defines it as estimated face height ([ForecastTable.tsx:242-270, 429](../src/components/ForecastTable.tsx)). The **~1.5 m the user saw matches the unscaled GFS primary swell, 1.46–1.54 m at 11.8 s**, and surf-forecast's daytime value. On the run fetched 18 Nov at 13:20, the app's own Swell row reads slightly higher, 1.7–1.8 m, because it scales that train up to MFWAM ([openMeteo.ts:115-123](../src/lib/openMeteo.ts)). The user's 1.5 m may come from an earlier cached run.

A plausible alternative explanation can be ruled out: trade-wind sea is not inflating the number. Adding a ~2 m NE wind sea to a 1.5 m swell would push total Hs to about 2.5 m ([NWS marine glossary](https://www.weather.gov/jax/marine_glossary)). On 19 Nov, though, the models give **no wind sea before 14:00 and only ~0.5 m at 3.5 s after**. With the app's 0.7 weight, it adds about 1 % to the estimate.

## Three modelling choices add 0.3–0.7 m over Surfline

Getting from a 1.5 m swell to 2.8 m takes four steps, all in [conditions.ts:42-95](../src/lib/conditions.ts) and [openMeteo.ts:106-140](../src/lib/openMeteo.ts). First, Komar–Gaughan converts each swell train's offshore height into a breaking height, Hb = 0.39·g^0.2·(T·H²)^0.4 ([Komar & Gaughan 1972](https://scilit.com/publications/c519f98275ffa892262a9da63d1d0575)). That turns **1.5 m at 12 s into 2.29 m (×1.53)**, and the reef gain of 0.77 brings it back to 1.77 m. This step is legitimate physics. Surfline's own rule of thumb says face height for 12–16 s groundswell grows to about **1.3× deep-water swell height** at an average spot ([Surfline blog](https://www.surfline.com/surfline/forecasts4/forecast_blog_entry.cfm?id=22495&sef=true)). Second, the 1.1 m, 15–16 s NW secondary and a 0.45 m N tertiary are added as a root-sum-square of their own Komar–Gaughan heights, which lifts the estimate to 2.43 m (+37 %). Most of that is real energy: Surfline lists a 1.3–1.4 m, 12 s second swell as well. Third, every GFS train is multiplied by MFWAM total Hs ÷ GFS total Hs (×1.09–1.23), giving **2.56–2.86 m**. Fourth, exposure stays at 1.0, because the spot's ±70° window around 340° passes all three swells at full strength ([seedSpots.ts:197-223](../src/data/seedSpots.ts)).

Two conclusions follow. First, the **choice of reference model is the largest single error**. MFWAM is 15–18 % above ECMWF WAM and 9–23 % above GFS-Wave all day, and the code always takes it first (`waveH = hsMF ?? ec ?? gfs`) instead of a median. A 0.3 m spread between models is ordinary near coasts. Coastal validation of MFWAM shows a **scatter index of about 19–24 % and an RMS error of about 0.35 m** against buoys, versus about 8 % in open ocean ([CMEMS QUID GLO-WAV-001-027](https://documentation.marine.copernicus.eu/QUID/CMEMS-GLO-QUID-001-027.pdf); [CMEMS SQO](https://documentation.marine.copernicus.eu/SQO/CMEMS-GLO-SQO-001-032.pdf)). Second, even after fixing the reference model and the combination method, the estimate sits around 2.2–2.5 m: still right at the user's limit and at the top of Surfline's range. What remains is **Majanicho's exposure**. The app samples an open-ocean cell several km offshore ([geo.ts:71-75](../src/lib/geo.ts)) and gives Majanicho the same 0.77 gain as a fully open north-shore reef ([conditions.ts:38-40](../src/lib/conditions.ts)). That gain was not calibrated against observed surf. Decision 0011 set it only so that peak-period heights would match the older mean-period heights ([0011-gain-recalibration.md](../context/decisions/0011-gain-recalibration.md)). The repo's own spot research already flagged Majanicho as more sheltered than its neighbours. surf-forecast calls it "too small" on 14 % of days versus 2–5 % for exposed reefs, and local guides say the inside needs about 2 m on the outside reefs before it works ([local-surf-school-spot-knowledge.md](../context/research/local-surf-school-spot-knowledge.md), citing [FuerteventuraPlayas](https://www.fuerteventuraplayas.com/es/spot/majanicho-inside/) and [surf-forecast](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Majanicho)). The three peaks have different size thresholds, but all of them get the same spot-wide `hb`. The inside-right peak, with maxSize 1.9 m, is therefore judged against the outside reef's size.

Uncertainty remains on several points. No buoy observation or logged session exists for 19 Nov, so nobody yet knows whether Majanicho surfs at 2 m or 2.8 m tomorrow. Surfline is a better benchmark than the swell-only sites, but it is still a model. By Surfline's own published figures, its 4–6 ft forecasts landed within 1.5 ft only **about 62 %** of the time ([Surfline Labs](https://medium.com/surfline-labs/surf-forecast-accuracy-b563605f104c)). The surf shop's recommendation probably reflects local knowledge of the sheltered inside peaks and a broader audience than a stage-≤5 surfer with a 2.2 m ceiling. What the shop had in mind is not documented.

## A hard veto on a single estimate decides the outcome

Because of the inflated height, the app warns about Majanicho. Because of the decision logic, it excludes the spot. `suitability()` rounds `hb` to 0.1 m and compares it with M = max(Biggest caught, 1.2 × Comfortable). For a stage-≤5 surfer, anything above 1.15·M is `unsuitable`, and so is anything above a peak's maxSize × 1.15 ([rating.ts:188-226](../src/lib/rating.ts)). `recommendSpotDay` then sets the score to 0 for any unsuitable hour, whatever the user's past sessions say ([recommend.ts:257-260](../src/lib/recommend.ts)), and Explore ranks the spot below every suitable one with a ✕ ([Explore.tsx:217, 261-270](../src/components/Explore.tsx)). With M = 2.2 m, every daylight hour, 2.6–2.9 m, fails at all three peaks.

The rest of the app already knows the number is uncertain but never uses that knowledge. The ±20 % range (`hb × 0.8 … × 1.2`) is formatted text only ([format.ts:12-24](../src/lib/format.ts)). The confidence score `sureness()` looks at spot knowledge, wind-model spread and past sessions, but **wave-model spread is not an input anywhere** ([confidence.ts:85-107](../src/lib/confidence.ts)). At noon, a low end built from ECMWF instead of MFWAM is 0.8 × 2.52 = **2.0 m, under the limit**. A 2.2 m hard cut sits well inside typical coastal model error, so a spot forecast at 2.6 m has a real chance of surfing under 2.2 m, and the reverse.

The wider field handles this differently. No surveyed product hides a spot because forecast size exceeds a user setting. Surfline and surf-forecast give one rating and leave suitability to the user. Personalised apps such as BUIO, SurfMap and WaveCheck describe scoring and alerts, not exclusion ([SurfMap](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6749829392); [BUIO](https://buioapp.com)). Operational wave forecasting checks thresholds as **probability and margin together**. Being 90 % likely to exceed by 10 cm is a different risk from being 90 % likely to exceed by 1 m ([UN Ocean Prediction wiki](https://unoceanprediction.org/resources/wiki/chapters/380); [NOAA GWES](https://ocean.weather.gov/gwes_explain.php)). Weather-communication studies find that showing uncertainty **improves decisions and preserves trust**, while a bare recommendation does no better than a single deterministic value ([LeClerc & Joslyn, AMS 2011](https://ams.confex.com/ams/91Annual/webprogram/Paper182589.html); [arXiv 1408.4812](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4812)). The repo's own research had already warned against hard steps at band edges ([local-surf-school-spot-knowledge.md](../context/research/local-surf-school-spot-knowledge.md)).

## Swell should cross-check face height, not replace it

The user's instinct is right in one respect: when the computed heights are unreliable, the app should lean on the swell forecast. The industry pattern is swell first, then a spot transfer, then wind and tide. Surf Captain, for example, builds surf height from the **two most energetic swell partitions** at each spot's offshore point ([Surf Captain](https://surfcaptain.com/blog/1/understand-the-surf-height-forecast)). Still, comparing raw swell height with the user's 2.2 m face-height limit would be wrong, for three reasons. The two numbers are on different scales: a 1.5 m, 12 s swell already produces about a 2.3 m face at a fully exposed break. A swell-only estimate (1.72 m at noon) would also drop a 1.1 m, 15–16 s groundswell that Surfline confirms and that "sets hit harder than the height suggests". That errs toward under-warning, the dangerous direction. And swell partitions are not the more reliable inputs. Partition magnitudes are the weaker part of wave-model output, with swell reproduced less well than wind sea ([Hanson et al. 2009](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Hanson_etal_JTECH2009.pdf); [Stopa et al. 2016](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Stopa_etal_OM2016.pdf)). On 19 Nov, MFWAM's single swell partition is **2.2–2.3 m while GFS splits the same sea into 1.5 + 1.1 + 0.45 m**.

The better use of swell is as a second, independent size signal: **energy in the spot's window (∝ H²·T per partition)**. `deriveHour` already computes it as `eSum` ([conditions.ts:89](../src/lib/conditions.ts)). surf-forecast recommends that surfers note the energy on their smallest surfable and biggest comfortable days and use those as personal limits ([surf-forecast FAQ](https://www.surf-forecast.com/pages/faq)). A surf journal is built to do exactly that from logged sessions. When face-height sources disagree, the app can shift weight toward this energy signal, compared against the user's own logged history at that spot, instead of trusting one inflated face height.

Items 1–3 fix the decision problem without settling which height is "true", and together they would have kept Majanicho on the list with an honest warning. Items 4 and 5 fix the bias itself, but they need observed sessions to tune, or they will only move the error somewhere else. Per the repo rules, these changes require a `CONDITIONS_VERSION` bump, a `SEED_VERSION` bump for the gain change, and a decision record in `context/decisions/`.

## Conclusion

The discrepancy started as a definitions problem and became a decision problem. Comparing the app with surf-forecast is comparing a face height with an offshore swell height. Against Surfline, the one like-for-like benchmark, the app runs a few tenths of a metre high because it always uses the highest wave model and treats a sheltered reef as fully exposed. That bias alone would have produced a warning. The exclusion came from treating a number with ±0.4 m of normal coastal error as exact, in a one-sided rule that ignored the app's own uncertainty band.

The question of whether to "use swell instead of wave height" therefore has the wrong framing. The useful question is how much the app knows about the size at the break. When the models agree and the user has logged the spot, a calibrated face height should drive the recommendation. When they disagree, as on 19 Nov, the app should show the range, fall back toward swell energy measured against the user's own history, and keep outright exclusion for cases where even the optimistic estimate is too big. That direction also suits a journal app: every logged Majanicho session with an observed size makes the next forecast more accurate.
